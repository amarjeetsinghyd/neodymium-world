Air Marshal Dixit: Charting India's Airpower Future with Drones and Jointness

Amarjeet Singh Senior Analyst
Oct 06, 2026 8 Min Read

Strategic Intelligence Desk: Curated and verified by Senior Analyst Amarjeet Singh. Directed toward defense sovereignty, Indo-Pacific deterrence, and critical emerging technologies.

Key Takeaways

  • • The IAF under Air Marshal Dixit will intensify its focus on Beyond Visual Range (BVR) combat, leveraging advanced platforms and tactics for aerial superiority.
  • • Unmanned Aerial Systems (UAS) are poised for rapid integration into core IAF doctrines, transitioning from support roles to primary means of warfare.
  • • Dixit's leadership will reinforce the drive for greater tri-service jointness and multi-domain integration, building on his tenure as Chief of Integrated Defence Staff.
  • • His extensive operational experience across diverse aircraft and command roles will likely translate into pragmatic, technology-driven strategies for border deterrence and regional power projection.

A New Era for Indian Airpower Takes Flight

New Delhi, 31 October – Air Marshal Ashutosh Dixit assumes command as the new Chief of the Air Staff (CAS) today, marking a pivotal moment for the Indian Air Force (IAF). His ascension, succeeding Air Chief Marshal A.P. Singh, is not merely a change of guard but signals a profound strategic recalibration within India's aerial defense establishment. As the first officer to have commanded a Mirage-2000 squadron to reach the apex of the IAF, Air Marshal Dixit embodies a generation that fundamentally modernized Indian air warfare, placing a premium on beyond-visual-range (BVR) combat and increasingly, the integration of autonomous systems.

This appointment underscores a clear strategic trajectory for the IAF: a future where technological superiority, BVR dominance, and the seamless integration of manned and unmanned platforms will define India's airpower posture. His vision, articulated during his tenure as Chief of Integrated Defence Staff (CISC), positions robotic warfare not as a distant concept but as an immediate reality, critical for enhancing India's deterrence capabilities across its contested borders and projecting influence within the complex South Asian geopolitical theatre.

The Mirage-2000 Legacy: Engineering BVR Superiority

Air Marshal Dixit's deep connection to the Mirage-2000 is more than a biographical detail; it is a testament to his formative years coinciding with a transformative period for the IAF. The induction of the Mirage-2000 in the mid-1980s heralded a new era of air combat for India, introducing advanced avionics, sophisticated electronic warfare suites, and crucially, true BVR engagement capabilities. This platform, which Dixit re-equipped No. 9 Squadron with and commanded, laid the groundwork for the IAF's modern fighter doctrine, moving away from close-quarters dogfighting towards engagements at extended ranges.

His extensive flying experience, exceeding 3,500 hours across a diverse fleet including MiG variants, Jaguar, and Tejas, reinforces a pragmatic understanding of both legacy and contemporary platforms. This background suggests a leadership focused on maximizing the BVR potential of India's current fleet, including the Rafale and Su-30MKI, while simultaneously pushing for the next generation of indigenous BVR missiles and sensor integration. The imperative for BVR superiority remains paramount in countering sophisticated aerial threats from adversaries, demanding continuous innovation in radar, missile technology, and networked warfare capabilities.

Robotic Skies: Integrating Unmanned Systems into Warfare

Perhaps the most defining aspect of Air Marshal Dixit's strategic outlook is his unequivocal advocacy for unmanned systems. In May, while serving as CISC, he declared that "the robotic warfare of the future was already here," asserting that unmanned aerial systems (UAS) were no longer mere enablers but "means of warfare in their own right." This bold pronouncement signals a radical shift in doctrinal thinking, moving beyond conventional support roles for drones to envisioning them as integral components of frontline combat operations, potentially replacing manned aircraft in an increasing variety of roles.

This vision suggests a significant push for accelerated indigenous development and acquisition of advanced UAS, ranging from high-altitude long-endurance (HALE) platforms for persistent intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance (ISR) to loyal wingmen and combat drones capable of autonomous strike missions. The integration challenges are immense, encompassing secure data links, artificial intelligence for decision-making, and robust counter-UAS capabilities, but Dixit's stance indicates these will be priority areas for the IAF. This strategic pivot is vital for operating effectively in contested airspace, reducing risk to manned platforms, and achieving asymmetric advantages in future conflicts.

"Unmanned aircraft were 'already replacing manned aircraft in an increasing variety of roles' and were influencing the outcome of conflicts."

Forging Tri-Service Synergy: The Jointness Doctrine

Beyond the technological imperative, Air Marshal Dixit's tenure as CISC provided a critical platform for advancing tri-service integration, a cornerstone of India's ongoing defense reforms. During his time, he championed greater jointness, leading to the issuance of multiple joint doctrines and orders, and undertaking several initiatives to strengthen the armed forces' capabilities across domains. This experience is invaluable as India continues its push towards theaterisation, aiming to create integrated theater commands for more cohesive and effective military operations.

Under his leadership, the IAF is expected to play a crucial role in operationalizing these joint doctrines, ensuring seamless coordination with the Army and Navy for multi-domain operations. This includes integrated air defense, joint strike planning, and shared intelligence frameworks. The emphasis on jointness is not merely an administrative reform; it is a strategic necessity for India to effectively respond to complex, multi-front challenges, optimizing resource allocation and maximizing combat power through synergistic application across land, sea, air, space, and cyber domains.

Operational Acumen and Strategic Direction

Air Marshal Dixit's career is replete with diverse operational and command experience, providing a robust foundation for his strategic vision. From commanding a frontline fighter air base in the Western Sector to a premier fighter training base in the Southern Sector, and holding key appointments such such as Air Defence Commander of Southern Air Command and Deputy Chief of the Air Staff, he has garnered a comprehensive understanding of the IAF's operational requirements and strategic challenges. His involvement in operations like Safed Sagar and Rakshak further underscores his battle-hardened perspective.

This blend of technical expertise as an Experimental Test Pilot, operational command, and strategic planning roles positions him uniquely to steer the IAF through its modernization drive. His focus on indigenous platforms like the Tejas, coupled with his previous role as Principal Director, Air Staff Requirement, aligns perfectly with the Atmanirbhar Bharat (Self-Reliant India) initiative. The IAF under Dixit is likely to prioritize investments in domestic defense manufacturing, aiming to reduce reliance on foreign suppliers for critical technologies and systems, thereby enhancing India's strategic autonomy and industrial base.

Charting India's Airpower Trajectory Under Dixit

Air Marshal Ashutosh Dixit's appointment ushers in a new chapter for the Indian Air Force, one defined by a clear-eyed embrace of advanced technology and integrated warfare. His leadership will undoubtedly accelerate the IAF's transformation into a more agile, technologically sophisticated, and integrated fighting force. The emphasis on BVR combat, the aggressive integration of unmanned systems, and a steadfast commitment to tri-service jointness will be the hallmarks of his tenure.

As India navigates an increasingly complex regional security environment, a modern and potent air force is indispensable for deterrence and defense. Air Marshal Dixit's vision, rooted in his extensive operational experience and forward-looking strategic insights, positions the IAF to meet these challenges head-on, ensuring India's skies remain secure and its airpower capabilities remain formidable on the global stage.
