Corruption Probe at SMPP Exposes Vulnerabilities in India's Defense Ecosystem

Amarjeet Singh Senior Analyst
Oct 15, 2026 7 Min Read

Strategic Intelligence Desk: Curated and verified by Senior Analyst Amarjeet Singh. Directed toward defense sovereignty, Indo-Pacific deterrence, and critical emerging technologies.

Key Takeaways

  • • The CBI arrest of SMPP's CEO triggers strict debarment procedures under the Defence Procurement Manual (DPM) 2025.
  • • SMPP's operational footprint spans critical defense verticals, including 186,000 armor jackets, 125mm/155mm ammunition, and jet-powered kamikaze drones.
  • • Past supply chain shifts—such as transitioning from US to Chinese boron carbide components—underscore persistent material vulnerabilities.
  • • The Ministry of Defence faces a strategic dilemma in punishing corporate misconduct without stalling frontline readiness along contested borders.

The Anatomy of a High-Stakes Procurement Crisis

When the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) interdicted a hawala-based bribery pipeline this week, arresting SMPP Limited Chief Executive Ashish Kansal alongside Anil Kumar, a senior executive director at state-owned Munitions India Limited (MIL), the operational fallout extended far beyond standard anti-corruption enforcement. The case strikes at the heart of India's private sector defense manufacturing apparatus. For a firm that successfully transitioned from a modest pulp-based packaging unit in Sangrur, Punjab, to an essential private supplier of specialized ordnance and ballistic protection, the allegations threaten an abrupt operational freeze.

The Department of Defence Production (DDP) within the Ministry of Defence (MoD) is now moving to execute statutory protocols that could effectively isolate SMPP from the national defense market. Under the stringent provisions of the Defence Procurement Manual (DPM) 2025, an investigation of this scale mandates immediate administrative scrutiny. The immediate policy lever is an interim suspension of business dealings, a regulatory mechanism designed to insulate procurement pipelines from compromised entities while formal inquiries proceed. Yet, because SMPP holds active contracts across frontline infantry protection, heavy artillery ammunition, and newly inducted autonomous strike platforms, the MoD faces a complex trade-off between anti-corruption compliance and frontline supply chain continuity.

From Industrial Packaging to Heavy Ordnance

Founded in 1985 by Dr. Shiv Chand Kansal, a chemical engineer from IIT Bombay, SM Pulp Packaging Private Limited originally manufactured industrial paper containers. The company's strategic pivot began in the late 1990s when it developed specialized manufacturing capabilities for semi-combustible cartridge cases (SCCC) for 120mm and 125mm tank ammunition. This niche chemical and materials engineering expertise positioned SMPP as an indispensable tier-supplier to state ordnance factories, establishing a foothold in India's tightly controlled military industrial base.

The company's major commercial breakthrough occurred in 2018, when it secured a watershed contract valued at over ₹630 crore to manufacture 186,000 bullet-resistant jackets for the Indian Army. Competing against established defense contractors, SMPP leveraged advanced boron carbide ceramic composite plates—an ultra-hard material capable of defeating hard steel-core armor-piercing rounds while reducing overall weight burdens for infantry units operating in high-altitude operational theaters.

However, that contract also demonstrated the complexities of defense supply chain transparency. During initial competitive evaluation trials, SMPP submitted armor configurations utilizing raw materials and boron carbide plates sourced from established suppliers in the United States. Following the contract award, the firm shifted its raw material supply chains toward lower-cost Chinese vendors. While SMPP maintained that this material substitution was fully vetted and authorized by technical evaluation committees within the MoD, the pivot underscored latent vulnerabilities in domestic defense indigenization, where finished systems assembled within India remain reliant on foreign upstream supply chains.

Expanding into Ammunition and Jet-Powered Loitering Munitions

Undeterred by supply chain scrutiny, SMPP aggressively scaled its operations over the past five years, seeking to evolve from a protective gear manufacturer into a comprehensive armaments supplier. In 2021, the firm incorporated SMPP Ammunition Private Limited, establishing an industrial complex in Nalagarh, Himachal Pradesh. Backed by industrial licenses from the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT), the company laid plans to manufacture a wide spectrum of conventional munitions, including 30mm, 40mm, 81mm, 105mm, 130mm, 155mm, and 125mm tank shells.

To accelerate its technical capabilities in large-caliber ammunition, SMPP entered into a technical collaboration with Rheinmetall Denel Munition—a joint venture between Germany's Rheinmetall Waffe Munition GmbH and South Africa's state-owned Denel. Furthermore, the company expanded its footprint into unmanned strike systems. In June 2024, SMPP delivered a batch of 106 jet-powered kamikaze drones to the Indian Army under emergency procurement powers, providing high-speed tactical loitering strike options for strike corps deployed along contested border sectors.

  • Body Armor & Helmets: Large-scale supplier of boron carbide personal protection systems and tactical helmets to the Indian Army and central armed police forces.
  • Heavy Ammunition: Licensed manufacturer of 125mm tank rounds and 155mm artillery shells in partnership with international technology providers.
  • Unmanned Aerial Systems: Provider of jet-powered loitering strike platforms inducted under fast-track emergency procurement authority.

The Regulatory Mechanism of DPM 2025

The immediate strategic fallout centers on the mandatory mechanics of the Defence Procurement Manual 2025. Unlike legacy procurement guidelines, DPM 2025 establishes an automated, rigid protocol for handling vendors implicated in ongoing criminal investigations initiated by primary law enforcement agencies like the CBI.

Once an interim suspension order is issued by the competent authority—with ultimate decision-making power resting with the Defence Minister—the commercial consequences are swift and severe. Under DPM 2025, an entity under suspension is immediately disqualified from participating in any active or future Requests for Proposal (RFPs). In ongoing procurement tenders where Lowest Bidder (L1) determination has not been finalized, the firm's bid must be excluded, forcing technical evaluation committees to re-evaluate remaining commercial offers or cancel the tender outright.

While an initial suspension is capped at one year, subject to a mandatory administrative review within six months, it can be extended in six-month increments up to the maximum permissible duration of a full business ban. If the CBI files a formal charge sheet in court establishing corporate misconduct, the MoD can initiate full debarment proceedings across all defense departments. For a specialized vendor whose order book depends heavily on Ministry of Defence procurement, such administrative actions pose an existential commercial threat.

Navigating Supply Chain Continuity Under Atmanirbhar Bharat

The situation facing SMPP highlights broader structural dilemmas within India's defense indigenization initiative. As New Delhi aggressively expands the role of private enterprise to break the historic monopoly of Defence Public Sector Undertakings (DPSUs), regulatory frameworks must balance market integrity with strategic capacity building.

When a private vendor securing critical production lines in artillery ammunition or specialized loitering platforms faces debarment, the resulting operational gaps cannot easily be plugged overnight. Re-tendering contracts for complex armaments requires multi-year trial cycles, technical re-validations, and significant capital expenditure. As the Ministry of Defence processes the CBI's findings, South Block must navigate a difficult path: enforcing strict governance across the defense industrial base while ensuring that tactical readiness along India's northern borders remains uncompromised.
