Craft Prospect Launches PERSEUS to Pioneer Space Optical Comms

Amarjeet Singh Senior Analyst
Oct 27, 2026 7 Min Read

Strategic Intelligence Desk: Curated and verified by Senior Analyst Amarjeet Singh. Directed toward defense sovereignty, Indo-Pacific deterrence, and critical emerging technologies.

Key Takeaways

  • • The PERSEUS project evaluates Photonic-Crystal Surface-Emitting Lasers (PCSELs) for deployment in harsh orbital environments.
  • • The initiative is backed by the Advanced Connectivity Technologies (ACT) programme, delivered by Innovate UK and funded by the DCMS.
  • • Glasgow-based Craft Prospect is leading the effort alongside Fraunhofer CAP and Vector Photonics.
  • • Testing protocols include radiation, vibration, thermal-vacuum campaigns, and long-range free-space data transmission demonstrations.

The Orbital Bandwidth Bottleneck

Modern satellite architectures face an escalating data bottleneck. As constellations scale across low Earth orbit (LEO) and geostationary arcs, legacy radio frequency (RF) bands and first-generation optical systems struggle to keep pace with soaring throughput demands. High-resolution Earth observation, real-time tactical intelligence, and distributed satellite processing require a generational leap in free-space communications. Without robust alternatives to current bandwidth caps, tactical decision-makers risk operating on severely congested networks during critical operational windows.

Enter the Photonic-Crystal Surface-Emitting Laser, a disruptive semiconductor laser architecture promising unprecedented beam control and power scalability. While laboratory benchmarks for PCSELs have steadily climbed, bridging the chasm between controlled cleanroom environments and the unforgiving thermal, radiation, and vibrational realities of launch and orbit remains a formidable engineering hurdle. Solving this transition is the core mission of the newly unveiled PERSEUS project, bringing together specialized defense and photonics talent to rewrite the rules of orbital connectivity.

Inside the PERSEUS Architecture

Formally titled PCSEL Environmental and Radiation Sensitivity: Evaluation for Use in Space, PERSEUS is a collaborative enterprise uniting Glasgow-based space engineering firm Craft Prospect, the Fraunhofer Centre for Applied Photonics (Fraunhofer CAP), and Vector Photonics. The initiative is funded through the Advanced Connectivity Technologies (ACT) programme, delivered by Innovate UK and backed by the Department for Culture, Media and Sport (DCMS). Minister Ian Murray formally unveiled the overarching ACT R&D framework during his address at Connected Britain, signaling acute governmental backing for sovereign advanced communications capabilities.

Under the project roadmap, engineering teams are tasked with validating whether PCSEL technology can reliably survive and operate within high-radiation and wide thermal fluctuation environments. By moving beyond theoretical capability, PERSEUS seeks to demonstrate definitive compatibility with representative spacecraft hardware. The initiative effectively lays the groundwork for commercial solutions capable of supporting longer-range, highly secure, multi-gigabit free-space links between ground stations, orbital assets, and future deep-space nodes.

"By carrying out all the necessary early-stage work to test and characterise PCSELs for space, we will build a strong understanding of the potential that the technology offers in building the next generation of communication solutions."

Rigorous Validation Across the Spectrum

Transitioning novel photonics from benchtop to bus requires punishing test campaigns designed to simulate the violent ascent profile and the perpetual decay vectors of low Earth orbit. The PERSEUS consortium is subjecting PCSEL devices to exhaustive characterization protocols, encompassing thermal-vacuum chambers, proton and gamma radiation exposure, and multi-axis vibration testing. These empirical datasets will provide unprecedented clarity on component survivability and degradation metrics over multi-year mission lifespans.

Beyond isolated component stress tests, the program incorporates long-range, high-bandwidth free-space demonstrations to validate early operational performance. Project engineers are actively demonstrating early integration success with flight-representative subsystems, ensuring that end-to-end data transmission architectures can seamlessly harness PCSEL technology. These trials bridge the critical gap between raw optical component innovation and deployable, mission-ready space systems.

Forging the Roadmap for Smart Secure Space

The institutional pedigree behind PERSEUS ensures that the generated findings will directly influence upcoming European and Western satellite communications standards. At Craft Prospect, Head of Engineering Kevin Quillien directs the project as Lead, working alongside Chief Engineer Paul Yarr and Systems Engineers Lauryn Atkinson and Kairavi Pandit. Technical execution is further anchored by Dr Gerald Bonner leading the Fraunhofer CAP subcontract, alongside Dr Richard Taylor and Project Manager Dr Graham Goldberg steering Vector Photonics' proprietary high-volume manufacturing innovations.

This initiative builds directly upon Craft Prospect’s expanding portfolio in quantum-optical communications, including their ongoing contributions to the OPS-SAT VOLT mission. Through these integrated efforts, the team is actively developing and testing space-to-ground free-space quantum and optical links in alignment with their corporate vision of Smart Secure Space. As PERSEUS progresses, its data will directly inform future communications system design concepts, establishing a concrete engineering roadmap for sovereign optical dominance in the orbital domain.
