SLINEX-26: Anchoring India's Maritime Strategy in a Contested Indian Ocean

Amarjeet Singh Senior Analyst
Sep 24, 2026 6 Min Read

Strategic Intelligence Desk: Curated and verified by Senior Analyst Amarjeet Singh. Directed toward defense sovereignty, Indo-Pacific deterrence, and critical emerging technologies.

Key Takeaways

  • • SLINEX-26 significantly boosts India-Sri Lanka naval interoperability for complex maritime operations.
  • • The exercise is a strategic response to evolving geopolitical dynamics and external influence in the Indian Ocean Region.
  • • India's indigenous defense capabilities, exemplified by INS Kavaratti, are central to its regional security strategy.
  • • Bilateral drills like SLINEX are crucial for fostering trust, sharing best practices, and anchoring collective security in the IOR.

Forging Maritime Steel in the Bay of Bengal

Off the coast of Visakhapatnam, the Indian and Sri Lankan navies recently concluded the sea phase of SLINEX-26, a bilateral maritime exercise that saw a flurry of high-intensity operations. From intricate seamanship evolutions and air operations to demanding night replenishment at sea and specialized forces drills like Visit, Board, Search, and Seizure (VBSS) and slithering operations, the 13th edition of SLINEX underscored a deepening commitment to maritime interoperability. This is not merely a routine naval drill; it is a critical strategic signal, a tangible reinforcement of New Delhi’s resolve to secure its maritime periphery and consolidate its influence in a rapidly shifting geopolitical landscape.

The successful execution of SLINEX-26, following a productive harbour phase of professional exchanges and cross-deck visits, transcends the tactical. It directly addresses the high-stakes implications of maintaining stability across vital sea lines of communication and projecting a credible, collaborative security architecture in the Indian Ocean Region (IOR). As external powers increasingly vie for strategic footholds, these exercises become indispensable tools for fostering mutual understanding, enhancing operational readiness, and collectively deterring destabilizing influences that threaten the region's economic lifelines and sovereignty.

The Chessboard of the Indian Ocean: Countering Beijing's Shadow

The Indian Ocean, a crucible of global trade and a theatre of intensifying great power competition, demands constant vigilance and robust partnerships. While the official statements on SLINEX-26 emphasize enduring friendship and shared commitment to a secure IOR, the strategic analyst cannot ignore the elephant in the room: China's burgeoning maritime presence and its 'string of pearls' strategy. Sri Lanka, with its strategically vital location astride key East-West shipping lanes, finds itself at the nexus of this geopolitical contest, having already navigated the complexities of debt diplomacy exemplified by the Hambantota port deal.

Against this backdrop, SLINEX-26 serves as a deliberate counter-narrative, affirming India's role as a net security provider and a preferred partner in the IOR. By engaging in sophisticated drills with the Sri Lankan Navy, India is not only enhancing its own readiness but also strengthening the capabilities of a key littoral state, thereby building collective resilience against potential coercion or undue influence. This aligns perfectly with India's broader MAHASAGAR vision (Maritime Agenda for Security And Growth for All in the Region), which prioritizes collaborative security frameworks over unilateral dominance, fostering a stable and inclusive maritime environment.

Precision & Persistence: Engineering Interoperability at Sea

The specific drills undertaken during SLINEX-26 offer a granular insight into the operational priorities of both navies. Night replenishment at sea, for instance, is a complex evolution that demands exceptional coordination and proficiency, essential for sustaining long-duration deployments and projecting power far from home ports. Similarly, Special Forces drills, including VBSS and slithering operations, are critical for anti-piracy, counter-terrorism, and maritime interdiction operations – missions that are increasingly relevant in the IOR's dynamic security environment.

The participation of INS Kavaratti, an indigenous Anti-Submarine Warfare (ASW) Corvette, alongside the Fleet Tanker INS Jyoti, from the Indian side, highlights India's layered approach to maritime security. Kavaratti's ASW capabilities are particularly pertinent given the proliferation of submarines in the region, including those operated by non-traditional partners. INS Jyoti, meanwhile, is the logistical backbone, extending the operational reach and endurance of the fleet – a non-negotiable asset for any navy aspiring to project influence across vast oceanic expanses. SLNS Sindurala, an offshore patrol vessel, complements these assets, providing valuable surveillance and coastal defense capabilities crucial for Sri Lanka's maritime domain awareness.

"SLINEX-26 is more than a bilateral exercise; it is a strategic investment in regional stability, a palpable demonstration of India's commitment to collaborative security, and a quiet but firm counterpoint to the growing complexities of Indian Ocean geopolitics."

India's Indigenous Edge: A Force Multiplier in the IOR

The inclusion of INS Kavaratti, a testament to India's burgeoning indigenous defense manufacturing capabilities under the 'Atmanirbhar Bharat' initiative, adds another layer of strategic significance to SLINEX-26. Relying on domestically built platforms reduces external dependencies, enhances strategic autonomy, and ensures that India's defense posture remains robust and tailored to its unique security requirements. This self-reliance is not merely about production; it is about sovereign decision-making and the ability to dictate one's own operational tempo without the constraints of foreign supply chains or geopolitical leverage.

For partner nations like Sri Lanka, witnessing India's indigenous prowess fosters confidence in New Delhi's long-term commitment and capabilities. It signals a reliable partner capable of sustaining its own security needs and, by extension, contributing meaningfully to regional security frameworks. These exercises, therefore, are not just about training; they are about trust-building, capability demonstration, and the subtle art of strategic communication, conveying India's intent and capacity to be a steadfast guardian of the Indian Ocean's peace and prosperity.

From Visakhapatnam to Colombo: Anchoring Regional Security

The enduring maritime partnership between India and Sri Lanka, conceptualized in 2005 and now in its 13th iteration, has evolved into a sophisticated forum for mutual growth and security. The professional exchanges, cross-deck visits, and high-intensity drills during SLINEX-26 reinforce the shared commitment to a rules-based order in the IOR. Such engagements move beyond mere symbolic gestures, translating into tangible improvements in interoperability, communication protocols, and a common operational picture, which are invaluable in real-world scenarios ranging from humanitarian assistance and disaster relief (HADR) to coordinated responses against maritime threats.

As the strategic currents of the Indian Ocean continue to swirl with increasing velocity, the importance of these bilateral and multilateral naval exercises cannot be overstated. They are the sinews of regional security, binding nations together through shared training, mutual respect, and a collective vision for a stable and prosperous maritime future. SLINEX-26, therefore, stands as a crucial waypoint in India's broader strategic journey, demonstrating its unwavering resolve to be a responsible and capable anchor of security in its maritime backyard.
