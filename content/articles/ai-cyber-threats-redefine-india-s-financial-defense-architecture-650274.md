<h2>AI Cyber Risk in Banking: India's Strategic Imperative</h2><p>The convergence of AI-powered cyber threats and India's digital financial infrastructure represents a critical national security vulnerability. I analyze how frontier AI systems like Claude Mythos compress vulnerability exploitation timelines from weeks to hours, creating systemic risk for India's banking sector.</p><h2>Expert Commentary</h2><p>The recent regulatory advisory elevating cybersecurity responsibility to board level reflects India's recognition that AI-enabled threats transcend technical domains. Banks must transform from reactive security postures to proactive AI defense architectures, integrating vulnerability assessment, penetration testing, and incident response under accelerated timelines.</p><h2>Strategic Forecasting</h2><p>I project India's banking sector will face three critical challenges: prioritization paralysis from AI-generated vulnerability overabundance, patch management dilemmas balancing speed with stability, and controlled access to frontier AI defense tools. The Crowdstrike incident demonstrates how corrective measures themselves can introduce systemic risk, a lesson Indian banks must internalize.</p><h2>Atmanirbhar Bharat Implications</h2><p>India's response must balance immediate defensive needs with long-term strategic autonomy. Small Language Models offer controlled alternatives for specific defensive tasks, but India requires indigenous AI defense capabilities to counter sophisticated state-sponsored threats targeting financial infrastructure.</p><h2>Geopolitical Context</h2><p>South Asian rivals increasingly leverage cyber capabilities for strategic advantage. India's banking sector vulnerability represents a force multiplier for adversaries seeking to destabilize the regional economy through financial disruption.</p>
