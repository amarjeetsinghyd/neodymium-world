<h2>Strategic Context</h2><p>I project India's BRICS Science, Technology and Innovation (STI) initiative as a masterstroke in 21st-century statecraft. It's not merely about collaboration; it's about constructing a geopolitical bulwark against technological hegemony. This platform enables India to project power and shape norms in a multipolar world.</p><h2>Expert Commentary</h2><p>Dr. Jitendra Singh's address reveals a clear strategic intent. The theme 'Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability' is a coded message for de-risking from Western supply chains. India is weaponizing cooperation to build a resilient tech bloc, ensuring critical innovations remain accessible and affordable for its strategic partners.</p><h2>Strategic Forecasting</h2><p>I forecast this BRICS STI alliance will accelerate the fragmentation of the global technology order. We will see a bifurcated system: one led by the US and its allies, and another by BRICS, with India as the de facto leader. This will force global corporations and nations to choose sides, creating significant market and security implications.</p><h2>Atmanirbhar Bharat in Action</h2><p>The minister's reference to India's National Quantum Mission and BioE3 Policy is telling. These are not just domestic initiatives; they are templates for the BRICS bloc. India is offering a proven model of state-led innovation that balances commercial success with national security, a model highly attractive to other developing nations.</p><h2>Implications for the Global South</h2><p>This strategy directly appeals to the Global South's desire for technological self-determination. By offering an alternative to Silicon Valley's model, India positions itself as the leader of a new technological non-aligned movement. This will erode the soft power and economic influence of traditional Western tech giants.</p>
