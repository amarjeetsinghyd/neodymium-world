<h2>Strategic Overview</h2><p>I assess the impending El Niño event as a strategic threat multiplier for India's national security framework. The NOAA's 81% probability of a very strong El Niño during October-December 2024, potentially ranking among the largest since 1950, signals severe disruptions ahead. This climate phenomenon directly impacts India's agricultural heartland, water security, and food distribution networks - all critical components of national defense infrastructure.</p><h2>Geopolitical Implications</h2><p>I project that India's defense posture must adapt to climate-induced resource scarcity scenarios. The historical correlation between strong El Niño years and deficient monsoon rainfall, particularly in 2014-2015 when India experienced its top-four driest years this century, demonstrates the recurring nature of this threat. These climate shocks create cascading effects on internal security, border management, and regional power dynamics that India's defense establishment must anticipate and mitigate.</p><h2>Expert Commentary</h2><p>I analyze the climate science through a strategic lens, noting that the Niño 3.4 index crossing 1.2°C indicates moderate El Niño conditions, with strong events defined as >1.5°C and very strong as >2°C. The 2015-2016 'Godzilla' El Niño, persisting for nearly two years with temperatures above 2°C for over four months, provides a concerning precedent. Climate scientist Roxy Mathew Koll's explanation of oceanic warming and weakened trade winds reveals the accelerating nature of this climate threat, which demands immediate strategic response from India's defense and security apparatus.</p><h2>Strategic Forecasting</h2><p>I forecast that the current El Niño event, beginning in June 2024 and predicted to continue through early 2027 with 97% probability, will fundamentally alter India's strategic calculus. The IMD's forecast of slightly below-normal rainfall until July 22, with western India experiencing significant reductions, signals immediate operational challenges. I project that 2027 could emerge as one of the hottest years on record, compounding existing climate vulnerabilities and creating unprecedented security challenges that will test India's Atmanirbhar Bharat initiatives and regional influence.</p>
