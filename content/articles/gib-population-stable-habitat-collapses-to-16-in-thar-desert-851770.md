<h2>Strategic Analysis of the Great Indian Bustard Crisis</h2><p>The Great Indian Bustard (GIB) population's stability at 130 birds is a misleading indicator of ecological health. This figure masks a catastrophic habitat collapse, with the species confined to only 16% of its potential Thar desert range. The situation represents a strategic failure in balancing India's renewable energy ambitions with biodiversity conservation.</p><h2>Expert Commentary</h2><p>I project this habitat restriction to intensify as renewable energy projects expand across the Thar landscape. The GIB's poor frontal vision makes it exceptionally vulnerable to power line collisions, a direct consequence of poorly planned energy infrastructure. The Supreme Court's December order to bury power lines in specific stretches is a necessary but insufficient corrective measure.</p><h2>Strategic Forecasting</h2><p>Without immediate, large-scale habitat protection and energy infrastructure redesign, the GIB faces inevitable extinction within a decade. The current captive breeding program at Sam and Ramdevra is a stopgap, not a viable long-term strategy. This crisis will likely become a geopolitical liability, impacting India's international environmental standing and conservation commitments.</p><h2>Threat Assessment</h2><p>Habitat fragmentation from power lines, agricultural fencing, roads, and solar plants is the primary threat. These developments create mortality hotspots and isolate remaining populations. The species' avoidance of human-modified landscapes makes it an indicator species for ecological degradation in the region.</p>
