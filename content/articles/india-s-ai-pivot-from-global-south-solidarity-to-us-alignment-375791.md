<h2>India's AI Crossroads: A Strategic Analysis</h2><p>I analyze India's recent AI governance shift as a critical juncture in its technological sovereignty journey. The country's pivot from Global South solidarity to US-aligned middle power positioning reflects deeper strategic calculations about technological development.</p><h2>Expert Commentary</h2><p>India's February 2026 AI Impact Summit initially positioned the country as a champion of Global South concerns. The summit's themes centered on real-world harms and equity, contrasting sharply with previous Western-dominated summits focused on existential risks.</p><p>However, the political momentum quickly shifted toward capital attraction and domestic adoption. India's subsequent alignment with Pax Silica represents a strategic departure from its original vision.</p><h2>Strategic Forecasting</h2><p>I project India's middle power positioning will create strategic vulnerabilities. The country's aspirations to join technological peers like Japan ignore fundamental capability gaps and economic realities.</p><p>The US's disinterest in multilateral AI governance raises fundamental questions about India's technological future. Without proper guardrails, India risks becoming a digital colony extracting resources for American Big Tech.</p><p>India must leverage its unique position at the UN Global Dialogue on AI to reassert technological sovereignty. The country possesses the political heft and market diversity to lead a new approach to AI governance.</p><h2>The Path Forward</h2><p>India should reject the consumer-only role and instead champion public-purpose AI development. The country must establish robust protections for communities affected by data center expansion and AI deployment.</p><p>Strategic autonomy requires India to develop indigenous AI capabilities while maintaining selective international cooperation. The balance between innovation and regulation will determine India's future in the global AI landscape.</p>
