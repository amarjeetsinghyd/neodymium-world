<h2>India Summons Meta Global Team Over Platform Failures</h2><p>The Indian government has summoned Meta's global leadership to Delhi for a critical meeting on August 5-6, 2026. This high-level engagement follows the wrongful restriction of Prime Minister Narendra Modi's recent Facebook post and persistent lapses in curbing Child Sexual Abuse Material (CSAM). The move signals a direct challenge to Meta's operational autonomy and a demand for systemic accountability.</p><h2>Expert Commentary</h2><p>IT Secretary S. Krishnan stated that a leading technology company like Meta should have systems that function correctly. He emphasized that the government will question Meta on the extent to which synthetically generated information is being monitored and the safeguards in place for prominent verified accounts. The government's position is clear: Meta's explanation of a technical glitch is insufficient for a company at the forefront of technology.</p><h2>Strategic Forecasting</h2><p>I project this meeting will be a pivotal moment in defining the future of Big Tech regulation in India. The government is not merely seeking an apology but a concrete roadmap for algorithmic transparency and compliance with Indian law. This action could foreshadow stricter regulations on all major platforms operating in India, with a focus on preventing content moderation errors that impact national security and public order.</p><h2>Key Issues on the Agenda</h2><p>The meeting will cover a range of concerns, including Meta's measures against CSAM in paid advertisements and the safeguards for content removal from prominent accounts. The government will also demand clarification on algorithmic bias and the company's role in maintaining public order. The summoning of global executives like Joel Kaplan underscores the Centre's intent to engage Meta's international leadership directly on these critical issues.</p>
