<h2>Kissht's Q1 Performance: A Growth Story with Cracks</h2><p>Kissht's 59% YoY profit surge to ₹95.1 Cr is a textbook case of growth masking underlying risk. The NBFC's operating revenue jumped 45% to ₹669.5 Cr, but this was fueled by a 42.2% YoY increase in expenses. The company's assets under management (AUM) crossed the ₹8,000 Cr mark, but this scale is built on a precarious foundation of unsecured lending.</p><h2>Expert Commentary</h2><p>I see a classic high-growth NBFC playing with fire. The 653 basis point YoY worsening of the Provision Coverage Ratio to 84.13% is the most alarming data point. For a lender with 92.3% of its book in unsecured personal loans, this is not a temporary blip but a structural problem.</p><h2>Strategic Forecasting</h2><p>I project Kissht's growth trajectory will sharply decelerate if defaults rise. The company's plan to scale its loans against property (LAP) offering is a necessary but insufficient corrective action. Without a fundamental overhaul of its underwriting standards, the current profit surge will prove to be a one-time event.</p><h2>The Unsecured Loan Trap</h2><p>The heavy reliance on unsecured personal loans is Kissht's Achilles' heel. A 2.25% gross NPA is deceptively low for this portfolio segment. The company's digital-first model, while efficient for customer acquisition, lacks the rigorous due diligence required for such high-risk lending.</p><h2>Future Outlook and Market Impact</h2><p>Kissht's recent IPO at a ₹926 Cr valuation now appears optimistic. Investors should scrutinize the PCR trend and the quality of the AUM. A sharp correction is likely if the market prices in the true extent of the credit risk embedded in the balance sheet.</p>
