<h2>Funding Landscape Transformation</h2><p>The Indian startup ecosystem is witnessing a fundamental restructuring as mega deals plummeted 55% in H1 2026. Investors are recalibrating their approach, moving away from funding growth at any cost toward demanding sustainable business models. This shift represents a maturation of the ecosystem rather than a decline in innovation potential.</p><h2>Expert Commentary</h2><p>I observe that investor discipline has increased dramatically following governance lapses across the startup ecosystem. Founders misrepresenting financials and repeatedly raising capital without demonstrating sustainable fundamentals have made late-stage investors far more discerning. This behavioral shift is creating a healthier funding environment where quality trumps quantity.</p><h2>Strategic Forecasting</h2><p>I project that the funding cycle is naturally evolving as investors who deployed capital during the 2021 peak are now prioritizing liquidity events over fresh capital deployment. With IPOs deferred and M&A activity declining, the venture capital cycle faces significant disruption. This creates both challenges and opportunities for startups, particularly those in strategic sectors aligned with national priorities.</p><h2>Sectoral Implications</h2><p>Deeptech and manufacturing sectors, historically underserved by venture capital, are positioned to capture a larger share of future mega funding rounds. Founder ambitions are shifting toward establishing India as a world-class manufacturing and export hub, with investor interest following this strategic realignment. Sectors aligned with national priorities—infrastructure, defense tech, aerospace, space tech, and AI infrastructure—will likely attract disproportionate investment attention.</p>
