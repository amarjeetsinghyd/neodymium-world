<h2>Analysis of a Preventable Tragedy</h2><p>The tragic events in Telangana represent a catastrophic failure of the justice system. A man accused of stalking a minor, who was granted anticipatory bail, used his freedom to commit multiple murders before taking his own life. This sequence of events was not unforeseeable and points to a breakdown in risk assessment and monitoring by law enforcement and the judiciary.</p><h2>Expert Commentary</h2><p>I see this as a direct consequence of a procedural system that prioritizes procedural rights over public safety. The accused was known to be a threat, yet he was released on a personal bond with minimal oversight. The system failed to connect the dots between the POCSO case and the potential for violence, a failure that cost six lives.</p><h2>Strategic Forecasting</h2><p>I project that such incidents will become more common as social tensions rise and law enforcement resources are stretched thin. Without immediate reforms to bail conditions and witness protection protocols, the state will continue to fail in its duty to protect its most vulnerable citizens. The public's trust in the justice system will erode further if these systemic flaws are not addressed with urgency.</p>
