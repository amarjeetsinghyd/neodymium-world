<h2>Political Fallout of Education Corruption Crisis</h2><p>The CJP protest over NEET paper leak represents a critical inflection point in India's governance landscape. What began as legitimate student grievances has morphed into a full-blown political crisis challenging the Modi administration's credibility. The movement's expansion beyond student circles to include farmers, activists, and opposition parties signals systemic failure in addressing public grievances.</p><h2>Expert Commentary</h2><p>I observe this protest as a symptom of deeper institutional decay within India's educational framework. The government's heavy-handed response, including internet shutdowns and lathicharges, demonstrates authoritarian tendencies that undermine democratic principles. The protesters' demands for minister's resignation reflect public frustration with systemic corruption that transcends party politics.</p><h2>Strategic Forecasting</h2><p>I project this crisis will intensify as opposition parties capitalize on public discontent. The government's refusal to address core demands while offering token talks indicates strategic miscalculation. Without substantive action on examination reforms and accountability measures, the protest will likely expand, potentially impacting upcoming state elections and the 2029 general election calculus.</p><h2>Geopolitical Implications</h2><p>This domestic crisis undermines India's soft power projection internationally. Foreign investors and educational institutions may question India's governance stability. The protest's connection to broader anti-establishment sentiment suggests potential for coalition building across ideological divides, creating unprecedented political challenges for the ruling establishment.</p>
