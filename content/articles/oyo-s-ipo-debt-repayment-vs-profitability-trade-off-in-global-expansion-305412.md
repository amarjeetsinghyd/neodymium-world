<h2>OYO's IPO: A Strategic Pivot in Global Hospitality</h2><p>I analyze OYO's rebranded PRISM IPO as a case study in strategic repositioning. After four years of IPO delays, the hospitality giant returns with a fundamentally different business model.</p><h2>Debt Burden Shapes IPO Strategy</h2><p>Nearly 75% of the ₹6,650 crore IPO proceeds will repay debt rather than fund expansion. This allocation reveals PRISM's priority: financial stability over aggressive growth.</p><h2>International Revenue Dominance</h2><p>84% of revenue now originates outside India, with the US and Europe driving 51% of total revenue. This international diversification represents a successful pivot from the original India-centric model.</p><h2>Profitability Achieved After Years</h2><p>PRISM reports ₹748 crore profit after tax for the first nine months of FY26, surpassing its full-year FY25 revenue. This profitability milestone validates the company's turnaround strategy.</p><h2>Expert Commentary</h2><p>I observe that PRISM's IPO structure differs significantly from typical startup listings. Unlike recent Indian IPOs where early investors exit, PRISM's offering maintains ownership concentration while strengthening the balance sheet.</p><h2>Strategic Forecasting</h2><p>I project that PRISM's debt repayment strategy will reduce annual finance costs by approximately ₹400-500 crore. This financial discipline should improve free cash flows and enhance return ratios, making the company more attractive to institutional investors.</p><h2>Governance Concerns</h2><p>The pledging of founder Ritesh Agarwal's shares as collateral raises governance questions. Market participants will scrutinize this arrangement, particularly given the interconnected ownership structure.</p>
