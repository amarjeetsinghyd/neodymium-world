<h2>OYO's IPO Gameplan</h2><p>PRISM has filed its updated DRHP for a third attempt at an Indian IPO, seeking a valuation of up to $8 Billion. The ₹6,650 Cr offering is a pure fresh issue, with no offer for sale (OFS). The primary use of proceeds, ₹4,987 Cr, is allocated to debt repayment, a critical move to de-risk the balance sheet before public market scrutiny.</p><h2>The Profit Story</h2><p>Unlike its previous failed attempts, OYO enters this IPO with a significantly stronger financial footing. PRISM reported a consolidated net profit of ₹748 Cr in the nine months of FY26, a nearly threefold increase from its full-year FY25 profit. Operating revenue also grew 11% year-on-year to ₹6,941 Cr, signaling a return to sustainable growth.</p><h2>The Global Shift</h2><p>PRISM is strategically repositioning itself as a global hospitality platform, not just an Indian story. The company's network now includes over 24,000 hotels and 125,000 homes, with Europe and the US as primary growth engines. In 9M FY26, overseas operations contributed over 83% of the top line, demonstrating the company's successful global diversification.</p><h2>Expert Commentary</h2><p>I project OYO's IPO will be a litmus test for investor appetite for Indian unicorns with complex global operations and significant legal entanglements. The shift to a pure fresh issue indicates management's confidence in using the capital to strengthen the core business rather than rewarding early investors. The heavy debt repayment focus is a necessary move to appease public market investors wary of leverage.</p><h2>Strategic Forecasting</h2><p>I forecast the IPO's success hinges on the market's assessment of the legal risks versus the financial turnaround. The Zostel dispute, which could force a 7% shareholding transfer, remains the single largest overhang. Furthermore, human trafficking lawsuits in the US and an antitrust penalty in India could create material liabilities. Investors will weigh whether the 3X profit surge and global expansion strategy are sufficient to justify the premium valuation.</p>
