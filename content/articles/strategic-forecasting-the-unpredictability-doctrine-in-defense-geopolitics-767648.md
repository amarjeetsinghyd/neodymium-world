<h2>Strategic Forecasting</h2><p>I project that the doctrine of control is obsolete in modern defense strategy. The source material's core theme—that we cannot control everything—is a critical strategic imperative. Nations that cling to the illusion of predictability will be outmaneuvered by those that build for adaptability.</p><h2>Expert Commentary</h2><p>The analysis of Mark Manson's work on control is directly applicable to statecraft. A nation's obsession with controlling every variable—be it a border skirmish or a technology pipeline—leads to strategic brittleness. True strength lies in the disciplined allocation of national power toward controllable responses, not in the futile attempt to dictate all outcomes.</p><h2>Strategic Forecasting</h2><p>I forecast a paradigm shift in Indian defense thinking. The next decade will see a move away from platform-centric, predictable warfare toward multi-domain, unpredictable conflict. Our strategy must be built on the resilience to absorb shocks, not the fragile hope of preventing them. This is the essence of strategic depth.</p><h2>The Atmanirbhar Bharat Imperative</h2><p>The pursuit of self-reliance in defense technology is a direct confrontation with uncertainty. I assert that a purely linear, controlled development model for critical technologies like AI and hypersonics is doomed to fail. We must build a flexible, decentralized innovation ecosystem that thrives on unpredictability, much like the 'Black Swan' theory suggests.</p>
