<h2>Executive Analysis</h2><p>The US Department of Homeland Security's recent policy change ending the flexible duration-of-status system for student visas represents a strategic challenge to India's defense technology ambitions. I project this policy shift will accelerate India's push for indigenous defense capabilities while simultaneously creating talent shortages in critical technology sectors.</p><h2>Strategic Implications</h2><p>The four-year visa limit directly impacts Indian students pursuing advanced degrees in defense-relevant fields like AI, quantum computing, and aerospace engineering. This restriction could force India to develop alternative technology transfer mechanisms and accelerate domestic research capabilities.</p><h2>Expert Commentary</h2><p>I observe that this policy change aligns with broader US strategic competition with China, potentially creating secondary effects for India's defense procurement decisions. The timing coincides with India's increased defense spending and technology indigenization efforts under Atmanirbhar Bharat.</p><h2>Strategic Forecasting</h2><p>I forecast India will respond by strengthening domestic defense education institutions and creating alternative pathways for technology acquisition. The policy shift may accelerate India's pivot toward Russian and European defense technology partnerships as alternatives to US systems.</p><h2>Market Impact</h2><p>The education technology sector faces immediate disruption, but defense companies will experience longer-term talent pipeline challenges. I project increased demand for domestic training programs and potential salary inflation for specialized defense technology professionals.</p>
